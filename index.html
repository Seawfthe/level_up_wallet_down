01 — The issue

A purchase you never feel

Buying a game used to mean handing over cash once. That is not how games make money any more.

Most free games earn in fragments: a skin here, a few gems there, a season pass every few months. Each payment is small enough to dismiss, and nothing physical changes hands, so there is no moment where the cost becomes real.

Teenagers are still learning to judge what a price actually costs them. The games are engineered to make that judgement as hard as possible — one tap, saved card, no receipt, no leaving the room.

The problem is not that students are careless with money. It is that the spending is built to be invisible.

Our project asks one question: if nobody can see the total, how would a student ever notice it?

What it can become

A 27-year-old spent HK$80,000–90,000 on loot boxes, and borrowed from finance companies to keep going.

03 — Why it happens

Six designs doing the work

None of this is an accident. Each pattern below is a documented technique, and each one targets the moment you decide not to spend.

01

Loot boxes and hidden odds

A 1% drop rate means, on average, 100 pulls for the item you want. Some games do not publish the number at all.

What it does: turns a price into a gamble, so nobody can work out the real cost up front.
02

First-purchase rewards

Nine of the eleven games doubled or boosted your first purchase. The first one is there to make the second one feel normal.

What it does: converts a one-off decision into a habit.
03

Limited-time offers

“Only 3 hours left” and “limited stock” make waiting feel like losing, even when the same skin returns next month.

What it does: replaces thinking time with urgency.
04

VIP tiers and subscriptions

Auto-renewing passes and VIP levels turn spending into status — and the monthly charge becomes background noise.

What it does: makes spending tied to identity, not to want.
05

One-tap purchasing

A saved card removes the ten seconds where you would normally change your mind.

What it does: deletes the moment of doubt.
06

Permanent promotion

Pop-ups on login, on the home screen, while files download. There is no quiet part of the game.

What it does: makes not spending an active choice.

05 — What you can do

Five things, all free

None of these require quitting your favourite game. They require one decision made in advance, instead of one made at 11pm.

1

Check the odds before you pay

If a game will not tell you the drop rate, that is your answer. If it will, do the maths: 1% means 100 pulls on average.

2

Set a monthly budget — a number, not a feeling

Write it down before the month starts. “A bit” is not a budget; HK$150 is.

3

Delete the saved card

Typing your details in again is the cheapest self-control you will ever buy. Most impulse purchases die in those ten seconds.

4

Use the 24-hour rule

Want it now? Want it tomorrow at the same price? Then it can wait 24 hours. Limited-time pressure is exactly what this rule defeats.

5

Track seven days

Every purchase, the amount, and what was happening just before. The pattern shows up faster than you would expect.
